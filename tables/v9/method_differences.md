# Key differences between the method families (v8 manuscript and supplement)

| Method family | Target estimand | Within-subject dependence | Small-sample device | Reference distribution | Returns | Typical failure with few clusters / short series | Source (v8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GEE, uncorrected sandwich | Marginal (population-averaged) log odds ratio | Working correlation (exchangeable, independence or AR(1)) as nuisance; robust sandwich variance | None | t with N - p df (normal in Table 3) | Estimate + Wald CI | Anticonservative: clearly above 7.5% at 242 main/M1c, 69 M6-M7 and 100 Copula-AR1 design points | Ms 2.4; Table 5; S14 |
| GEE + Mancl-DeRouen (MD) or Fay-Graubard (FG); also Kauermann-Carroll (KC), CR2 | Marginal (population-averaged) log odds ratio | As uncorrected GEE; level of FG changed <0.5 points across working correlations | Leverage-adjusted sandwich: MD (I-H)^-1, KC/CR2 (I-H)^-1/2, FG bounded inflation (b = 0.75) | t with N - p df (CR2 not with Satterthwaite df) | Estimate + Wald CI | Separated data: plausible estimate, no warning (FG-AR(1) failure, N = 10, T = 2); KC, MD exceed 7.5% often | Ms 2.4, 3.4, 3.5, 3.11, 4.1, 4.3; S4 |
| GLMM, Wald test, Laplace approximation (lme4) | Conditional (subject-specific); larger than marginal by about (1 + k^2 sigma^2)^1/2 | Normal random intercept, random slopes in M3-M5; conditional independence given random effects | None in main run; adaptive quadrature (10 nodes) examined post hoc, random intercept only | t with N - p df; LRT against chi-square(1) | Estimate + Wald CI on conditional scale | Laplace anticonservative (515 points above 7.5%); random slope unidentifiable at T = 2; ignores serial dependence | Ms 1, 2.4, 3.3, 3.4, 3.6, 3.10, 4.1, 4.4; S12 |
| Marginalized multilevel model (MMLB::mm) with convergence check | Marginal (population-averaged) log odds ratio | Likelihood: latent variable (SD may trend over time) plus first-order transition term | Check: boundary fit (nlm code not 1/2, or latent-SD slope < 0.001) replaced by converged fit without trend | t with N - p df | Estimate + Wald CI (model-based SE) | Boundary non-convergence: no result in 16% of M3-M5 fits (32% at T <= 3); conservative at N = 10 | Ms 2.4, 3.2, 3.4, 4.2; S16, S20 |
| Sign-flip score test | No estimate; tests zero marginal interaction via effective score of logistic model without interaction | Subjects treated as independent units; dependence absorbed in per-subject score sums | Randomization: all sign flips up to 12 subjects, else 1,999 random plus identity | Sign-flip (randomization) distribution | p-value only; inverted interval often unbounded at T <= 3 | Not computable when null fit diverges or scores vanish; power 4.2% at T <= 3, N <= 25 | Ms 2.4, 3.7, 4.1; S7 |

Notes. N = number of clusters (subjects); p = number of mean-model coefficients; T = maximum number of occasions; df = degrees of freedom; k = 16√3/(15π), σ² = random-intercept variance. "Clearly above 7.5%": lower limit of the exact 95% interval of the null rejection rate above 7.5%, over the 2,376 design points with a t reference (Table 5). With a t reference KC was clearly above 7.5% at 138-207 and MD at 20-63 design points across working correlations; FG with exchangeable correlation at 15 (§3.4, §4.3). KC counts use *fewclust* (published formula); *geesmv* computes a different quantity (S4). GLMM with random slopes at T = 2: usable Wald test in 0.002% of attempts (Table 4). Adaptive quadrature lowered the GLMM null rejection rate by 3.1-4.0 points but left excess at T = 10, mainly on Copula-AR1 (§3.10). MMM returned 4 usable results on separated datasets (infinite interaction estimate) in the whole study (§3.4). The shrinkage GLM (rescaled GLMM) and the GLMM likelihood-ratio test (no interval; above 7.5% at 412 design points) are not separate rows. Sign-flip test not computable e.g. when one arm has only zero outcomes (S7).
