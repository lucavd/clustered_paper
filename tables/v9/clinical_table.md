## Linking the clinical findings to the simulation

In both datasets the two marginal methods agree on the full data (cohort: 0.35 by the marginalized model, 0.38 by GEE; trial: 1.3 by both), while the mixed model returns a larger coefficient on its conditional scale (0.48; 2.1). This is the estimand gap of Section 1, not a disagreement about the data; with a patient variance of 3.5 the attenuation factor is about 1.5, as built into scenario M1c. The subsamples reproduce three simulation features: availability falls with fewer patients for every method; the uncorrected sandwich understates the spread at 10 to 15 patients (trial ratio 0.5 to 0.6); and the Fay-Graubard and Mancl-DeRouen corrections overstate it at 10 patients (ratio 2.2), the direction of their conservatism on the grid. The applications also expose what the simulation underplays. With richer mean models, the corrections often returned an estimate without a standard error and were usable in only 15% to 34% of 10-patient draws, against 47% to 78% for the other methods; with four or five coefficients in the simulation they lost little availability (Table 4). Model-based standard errors of the marginalized and mixed models fell short of the spread in both datasets; in the cohort, part of this reflects between-patient heterogeneity of the zone contrast (random-slope variance 10), which no simulated mechanism contains, and the robust standard error of the marginalized model was close (0.85 to 0.99). The applications cannot show type I error or coverage: the reference is the full-data estimate, not a known truth, and the mechanism is unknown. Nor can they count the separated-data false positives of GEE (Section 3.4), although 365 of 5,000 trial subsamples had an infinite contrast.
